 As stated by the National Institute of Health, at the base pair level, all humans share about 99.9% of their genome, meaning only 0.1% of our genetic make-up determines the difference between all humans.
 While this percentage may be seen as small, it is this 0.1% that determines our physical traits, how we respond to certain medications and treatments, and our genetic susceptibility to certain diseases. 

 However, our genome is not the only thing that determines how we act or appear in our lives. Epigenetics is the study of how our behaviors and our environment can cause changes in the expression of human genes. One of the most famous examples of how the human genome is not the only thing that defines us is the case of Mark and Scott Kelly. In this study, Mark and Scott Kelly were identical twin astronauts, with Scott being sent to space for 340 days while Mark remained on Earth. By using identical twins, it was understood that they both shared the exact same genome. However, when Scott returned from space, it became clear that environment can also define who we become as humans. When examined, it was found that Scott's epigenome shifted, meaning genes related to his immune response, bone density, and DNA repair began to express themselves differently. Within six months of returning to Earth, Scott's gene expression had returned back to normal, however it became clear that the environment plays a role in who we are.

 As such, this study helped us further understand that we are not defined by our genomes; instead, our genomes serve as the foundation for our biological make-up, but who we are as people is determined by the interactions between our DNA and environment.
