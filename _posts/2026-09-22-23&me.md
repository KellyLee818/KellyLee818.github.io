### Founding
23andMe was initially co-founded by Anne Wojcicki, Linda Avey, and Paul Cusenza in 2006, however when the company filed for bankruptcy in March of 2025, Anne Wojcicki claimed sole ownership by winning a $305 million bidding war through her organization, the TTAM Research Institute.   

### Initial Investment and Current Revenue
At the time of the founding of 23andMe, Ann Wojcicki was married to the co-founder of Google, Sergey Brin. As a result, Google contributed approximately $3.9 million to the company's initial funding, along with organizations such as Genentech, New Enterprise Associates, and Mohr Davidow Ventures. Additionally, funds were secured through grants, including from the National Heath Institute through the National Human Genome Research Institute. 

Following 23andMe's bankruptcy, the company is now considered a non-profit and has a current revenue of approximately $170 million. From recent data, this revenue has decreased drastically from its peak revenue of $299.5 million due to the nature of its product and inability to maintain a recurrent stream of revenue from its products. Furthermore, 23andMe's partnerships with other corporate research organizations have since expired, resulting in a loss of revenue stream. 

### Main Business Focus
The current main business focus of 23andMe is centered around widely accessible genetic testing to consumers at home. Through salvia-based DNA kits, customers are drawn in by the appeal of understanding their history, ancestry, and potential health risks. From the DNA data collected from the kits, 23andMe also works with research organizations and pharmaceutical companies to streamline drug discovery in relation to the biological insights found from consumers. 

### Future Success
At this point in time, the future success of 23andMe is widely debatable. As stated above, the organization has already filed for bankruptcy and is currently undergoing massive job cuts as a result of its declining revenue. Due to the failure of its DNA kits to also create a constant stream of revenue, 23andMe has shifted to focus on medical research. However, due to a data breach in 2023, the company is struggling to regain trust with its consumers, thus creating even more hurdles for the company's future outlook.
